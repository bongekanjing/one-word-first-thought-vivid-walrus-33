# Shortlist — "One Word, First Thought"

Twenty-four candidates, tested head-to-head against the safe answers a lexicographer
has already read forty times today (serendipity, ephemeral, petrichor, liminal,
luminous, whisper, gossamer, solstice, sonder, defenestrate).

The test for each row: put it beside those ten. Does it survive? A word only
survives if it names something a person could reach out and touch, and if its shape
in the mouth is strange enough to make a reader stop.

## The decision: **guddle**

*Guddle* (Scots and Northern English): to catch trout by groping with your bare hands
under the stones of a stream.

Why it wins:

1. **It is a verb with a body in it.** Not a mood, not an abstraction — a person
   kneeling in cold water, arm in a hole, fingers feeling along the underside of a
   rock. The judge does not have to be told a picture; the word is the picture.
2. **It names a vanished competence.** Poaching trout by hand is a whole piece of
   daily life that has dropped out of the language, and the word is the only thing
   left of it. That is exactly the "forgotten piece of daily life" a lexicographer
   is hungry for.
3. **The sound does the work.** The doubled *d* and the swallowed second syllable
   make the mouth fumble — you guddle the word out. It sounds like what it means
   without being one of the cute onomatopoeias everyone knows.
4. **It has a second, drier sense** — to make a mess of something, to muddle about —
   so it carries a small joke for anyone who knows it.
5. **It is unexpected but not noise.** Collins and Chambers both carry it. It is a
   real dictionary word, not a dialect curiosity dug out of a glossary, and it is
   six characters with no hyphen, space, or apostrophe.

Against *serendipity*: one is a greeting card, the other is cold hands and a fish.
Against *petrichor*: one has been Instagrammed to death, the other has never been
said aloud in a design studio.

## Rejected on policy, not on merit

Defenestrate, liminal, sonder, gossamer, luminous, solstice — all disqualified by
the rule against listicle words. Not bad words; just already spent.
